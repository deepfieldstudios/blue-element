Step two

Getting here & where to stay.

Dominica has two airports — most divers fly into Douglas–Charles (DOM), usually via a regional hub in the eastern Caribbean. Soufrière is on the calm south-west coast, about a 90-minute transfer from the airport.

  • We'll help you arrange an airport transfer

Step three

Before you dive.

Four things to sort ahead of time. None of it is heavy — but getting it done before you land means you're in the water on day one.

Health

Medical & fitness

Everyone completes a short medical questionnaire before diving. If you flag a relevant condition (heart, lungs, ears/sinuses, recent surgery, pregnancy), we'll ask for a doctor's sign-off — so check with your GP early if in doubt.

Cover

Dive insurance

Bring insurance that covers freediving and, ideally, hyperbaric treatment — DAN is the standard most divers use. Have your policy number to hand for your arrival form.

Pack

What to bring

Just swimwear, a towel and reef-safe sun protection. We provide all freediving equipment — but bring your own mask, wetsuit or fins if you have gear you love.

Level

Your recent numbers

Know your certification level and your honest recent personal bests and depths. It lets us plan the right progression and safety from your first session.

What to expect in the water

The calmest water in the Caribbean.

Soufrière Bay is a sheltered volcanic crater that drops steeply just metres from shore — which is why athletes train and set records here.

  • 26–30°C year-round (coolest March, warmest September)
  • 20–30m visibility
  • Almost no current — a sheltered bay
  • Deep water minutes from shore
  • Counter-ballast line & professional safety, every session
  • Consistent conditions — you rarely lose days to weather
